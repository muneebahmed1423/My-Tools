        The search for the right routine

        With the help of Allah Almighty, I've spent a lot of time thinking about what a day would look like if I lived it according to my meaningful, purposeful life. In other words, what would a day look like if I spent it chasing my dreams, attracting my goals and striving for them? I came up with plenty of theories, and they all pointed toward one. I wrote that one down and felt it was the theory I could use. But that day routine never clicked. The truth is something that clicks in your mind. You know it's true and you feel in harmony with it. This one didn't. So I kept thinking about it and asking what the right theory was and what a day would really look like. With the help of Allah Almighty, which definitely came, I think I finally landed on the right one. It was the same day routine I had followed when I was making my constitution for my life, the document called main document. The way I worked on that is the way I need to work on every other goal I have.

        2

        How main document got done

        So what did my days look like when I was creating main document? The bottom line is that all I thought about was that document. It was the only idea on the screen of my mind, and I held onto it through my will. The routine was just that idea playing out, the completion of main document. It meant that I took my papers with me everywhere and kept working on them. I remember going to my village for vacations with my documents in tow. I went to weddings with my documents in the car or in my bag, and a single piece of paper and a pen in my pocket. Any time I wanted to write something about main document, I'd just pull them out and start writing, even at a wedding. At school, an insight would sometimes pop up during class, and I'd write it down on the class register I had back then. Wherever I went, I only thought about this one thing. I still took care of everything else I was involved in, like school and weddings. I managed those. But the main thing I was driven to do was this document. Whenever I had any free time, I took out my pen and my copy and wrote about main document. That doesn't mean I didn't enjoy myself. I took breaks, listened to music and podcasts, and watched movies. But even while doing those things, my mind stayed on main document, and I always came back to it.

        3

        The attitude for every goal

        That's the attitude you need for all of your goals. According to your own theory about how to strive for goals, the journey starts with one thing. The very first step is planting the idea, your want, your goal, into your subconscious mind, and you do that by writing. So with your goals, this attitude would look like waking up in the morning and, before you even wash up, grabbing your register and the pen you keep by your pillow and writing about your goals for ten or fifteen minutes. But I'm not saying you have to write in the morning. What I'm saying is that, whatever your routine, whatever the time and whatever tasks you're busy with, all you're thinking about is your goals and planting them into your subconscious mind, because that's the first step. When that step is done successfully, you find yourself motivated enough to start taking action. I think the process is covered pretty thoroughly.

        3

        Cultivating a routine

        How a routine forms, and why some stay

        Most people never study this, yet they still build routines (we call them unaware people). Why do some routines last for years while others fade? Four factors decide it. Switch them on and off and watch the routine.

        The four factors · tap to switch on

        A student’s routine (wake up, get ready, travel, attend classes, return) ran for years, then disappeared the moment college ended. Emotional charge stayed, since they still miss it. But self-image, self-identity and focus were never tied to the routine itself. The need for the routine went away, so the routine went away.

        These four factors are the working explanation inside this theory. Their presence is not a literal guarantee that a routine can never change.

        The deliberate method of cultivating a routine

        Instead of leaving routines to circumstance, we build one with a precise method. First, two situations.

        X-type conditioning + X-type idea

        • The idea is harmonious with our conditioning, so there is no clash and no Chaos Vibration.
        • The idea is impressed, the body’s vibration is compatible, motivation appears, and we act. The first step is not something we have to force.
        • What decides how deep the routine grows is the involvement of the four factors.

        X-type conditioning + Y-type idea

        • This is the situation we actually care about. The Y-type idea clashes with X-type conditioning and produces Chaos Vibration: fear, hesitation, reluctance, no motivation.
        • The normal flow of motivation is disturbed, so we cannot wait to feel motivated.
        • The initial step must be taken deliberately. We have to force ourselves to initiate the action.

        Why is the first step always mine?

        • The fear is not necessarily about the Y-type idea at all. It is about my existing conditioning and its clash with the idea.
        • I may truly want it and know it is good for me, and still feel resistance. The difficulty of starting comes from the resistance, not from the activity.
        • Even with a harmonious idea I am the one who acts. The only difference is that motivation is already there. So in the X–Y situation, I take the first step despite Chaos Vibration.

        How to actually take action in the moment

        The aim is to make the work behind our goals part of everyday routine. That routine is made of single tasks, so the real question is small: how do I get up and do this one task while I feel fear, hesitation and reluctance? Whatever the answer is, it works for the second task, the third, and every one after.

        Before and during a task

        ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓

        The seven principles

        1Remember Al-WakilBefore the task · tap to open

        Right after the resistance appears, I remind myself: I remember, acknowledge and feel the name of Allah Almighty, Al-Wakil. Before the task I anticipate discomfort, embarrassment, insecurity and uncertainty.

        So I say to myself: Hasbunallahi wa ni’mal wakil. حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ

        Why do I say it? Because it gives me a feeling of protection before the task. Psychologically, I move from needing certainty before I act to being willing to act, while trusting that Allah Almighty will protect me through the uncertainty, embarrassment and discomfort I anticipate. I feel this protection, and I am willing to take the action.

        2My initiative will turn into a routineBefore the task · tap to open

        The step I am about to take is part of cultivating my routine. I already have the method: the sessions, the activity, the repetition.

        I remind myself that my initiative will contribute to the routine I want, then I leave the question of routine formation alone.

        3Understand the fear and turn a blind eyeBefore the task · tap to open

        The fear is connected to my X-type conditioning and its clash with the Y-type idea. It is not necessarily about the activity. It does not mean the activity is bad, or that I do not want it.

        Turn a blind eye: acknowledge the feeling, but refuse to let it decide an activity I have already judged worthwhile and safe. This means willingness to act despite discomfort, not disregard for real harm, punishment or responsibilities.

        4My intention is to engage in the activityBefore the task · tap to open

        I do not focus on whether I feel motivated, on the fear, or on how hard it might be. I focus on doing the activity itself: get up, prepare, begin the session. When it is done, move to the next.

        I look forward to it. I get excited and motivated towards it. My focus is on the action, not on the reluctance before it.

        5Affirm yourself for courageBefore the task · tap to open

        Courage does not mean the absence of fear. Courage means taking action despite fear. I do not wait to become fearless or comfortable.

        I tell myself: “Let’s just do this and see what happens. Jo hoga dekh lenge.” Whatever reasonable challenge comes, we will deal with it.

        6Drop the old routine with a full stopBefore the task · tap to open

        There is one more thing to understand: the problems I will face in implementing and cultivating this grit attitude and the routine based on it. There is really only one problem. I am currently caught up in X-type conditioning. When I try to leave it, it starts pushing me with urges to come back and go back to the activities tied to it.

        So I remind myself that the only way to build a new routine is to go through the process detailed above. On top of that, I have to drop the old routine completely, with a full stop.

        Wherever I am with that routine, whatever work or activity is still pending, and whatever task is unfinished, I leave it right where it is and am completely done with it. That gives me a totally clean slate, and then I start working on my new routine.

        I bring a sudden, complete halt to my old conditioning and my old routine.

        7Remember Al-LatifDuring the task · tap to open

        Now I am during the task. The pain I was anticipating is here, and the difficult situation is in front of me. I need strength and support to go through it.

        So again I remind myself: I remember, acknowledge and feel the name of Allah Almighty, Al-Latif. Allah Almighty is gentle, and He will treat me with gentleness considering the situation I am in.

        Psychologically, this gives me support and power to go through the discomfort, kind of like emotional support. So I go through it.

        The dua for ease

        When I do an action again and again, I expect it to become a habit. But before it turns automatic, before it becomes a habit, I am doing the activity through my will. That is difficulty, and that is pain. To be relieved of that pain, I need a dua.

        Why I make this dua

        The repetition is the part I do. The ease and the automatic nature are the part I ask Allah Almighty for. So while the routine is still being carried by my will, I ask Him to remove my distress and my pain, and to make this affair easy and automatic for me.

        The dua

        To be made while the activity is still being done through will, before it has turned into a habit.

        Problem Two: Lack of Persistence

        What persistence is

        Before looking at the solution, I first need to understand what persistence even is. Persistence is basically using will over and over. When will is used just once, there’s only one idea on the screen of my mind, and all my attention and effort is aimed at finishing one particular task, whether that’s namaz, a reprogramming session, or exercise. Doing that again and again is persistence.

        The screen of my mind · pick a task, then use will

        The one idea on the screenComplete: Namaz

        Procrastination is a one-time thing, and I can handle it with a decision. The bigger problem is staying consistent with that decision, and that’s where persistence comes in.

        How to deal with it

        There are two parts to persistence: desire cultivation and will. With a burning desire, there’s no lack of persistence, and will is what carries me through each repetition. I need both working together, and when they are combined, the behavior turns automatic.

        Desire cultivation

        What it is

        Desire cultivation is writing my goals with focus as if they’re already achieved, in simple present tense, like “I have passed my CA PRC level” or “I earn three lakh rupees a month.”

        Why it works

        Writing a goal as an already-achieved fact is what plants it as a desire rather than a wish. Desire is a feeling that pulls me toward a long-term goal, and that feeling comes from the subconscious mind, which is why focused writing works on it.

          Will

          What it is

          Will is simply holding on to a certain idea on the screen of my mind. In the case of persistence, all my attention and all my effort is directed to that idea, and that idea is completing a certain task, whatever task I have on hand.

          Repeated usage of will is persistence.

          Why Not Do the Activity Along with the R Session?

          The plan as a simulation

          Take an example where I have to exercise and I also have a reprogramming session, the R session. The first step is deciding when to start, meaning the exact time bracket. The second step is doing the activity within that exact time bracket, which completes the decision. The next day, I use will to do the same activity again, which is the repetition.

          What I’ll do instead

          I’ll do only the R session and nothing more. I won’t push myself to finish the exercise part, because the exercise part has to become automatic behavior. When a person takes action on something, it’s because the idea of that action is already sitting in the subconscious mind. So when I do the reprogramming sessions, I’m planting the action idea into my subconscious mind. Once it’s planted, I’ll be motivated to do the activity automatically, so I just have to wait for that.

          The mind diagram · the same mind, two plans

          Why the R session covers both conditions

          Condition 1 · Burning desire

          One part of persistence is burning desire. When a want gets planted into the subconscious mind, it turns into a desire. The R session does that.

          Condition 2 · Will

          I’m already using will for the R session itself. So both conditions for persistence are fulfilled, and that’s why I’d rather not do the activity alongside it.

          I’ll do the R session first for a few days. As my subconscious mind gets reprogrammed, that will start motivating me. Once it does, I begin doing the activity itself, it slowly becomes automatic behavior, and the condition for persistence is met too.
