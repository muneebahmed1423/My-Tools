        The Theory

        Routine Conditioning

        How the mind builds automatic behaviour, why a new goal feels hard to start, and the exact method for turning it into a routine. Hover or tap any part of a diagram to see what it does.

        1

        The mind, and what sits inside it

        Everything starts with the Mind Diagram: a circle split in two, with the body underneath. It shows how conditioning develops and why acting on something new can feel hard.

        Habit

        An idea that has become fixed in the subconscious mind and that we act upon.

        Conditioning

        A multitude of habits that produce our automatic behaviour. It is built over a lifetime, not born with us.

        X-type conditioning

        The existing habits we want to change. Neither good nor bad in itself: a paradise or a prison, depending on whether it supports our goals.

        Mind Diagram

        Existing conditioning sets our vibration, and our vibration decides which thoughts we attract.

        2

        How humans actually take action

        We take action when an idea is impressed upon the subconscious mind. Follow the chain below, step by step.

        The sequence of action

        › › › › ›

        Existing X-type habit

        The idea is already fixed in the subconscious, so the action happens automatically.

        New X-type idea

        New, but similar in nature to our conditioning. No conflict, so it feels natural and relatively easy, though not automatic.

        Actions become easy when the ideas behind them are harmonious with our existing conditioning.

        3

        When the new idea is different: Chaos Vibration

        We watch the world and start wanting something different for ourselves. That brings a Y-type idea: something we want to achieve, experience or become, but different in nature from our conditioning. It is impressed on a subconscious that already holds X-type habits. What happens?

        4

        How a routine forms, and why some stay

        When a multitude of habits show up in our everyday actions, that is our routine: conditioning expressed in daily life. Most people never study this, yet they still build routines (we call them unaware people). Why do some routines last for years while others fade? Four factors decide it. Switch them on and off and watch the routine.

        The four factors · tap to switch on

        A student’s routine (wake up, get ready, travel, attend classes, return) ran for years, then disappeared the moment college ended. Emotional charge stayed, since they still miss it. But self-image, self-identity and focus were never tied to the routine itself. The need for the routine went away, so the routine went away.

        These four factors are the working explanation inside this theory. Their presence is not a literal guarantee that a routine can never change.

        5

        The deliberate method of cultivating a routine

        Instead of leaving routines to circumstance, we build one with a precise method. First, two situations.

        X-type conditioning + X-type idea

        • The idea is harmonious with our conditioning, so there is no clash and no Chaos Vibration.
        • The idea is impressed, the body’s vibration is compatible, motivation appears, and we act. The first step is not something we have to force.
        • What decides how deep the routine grows is the involvement of the four factors.

        X-type conditioning + Y-type idea

        • This is the situation we actually care about. The Y-type idea clashes with X-type conditioning and produces Chaos Vibration: fear, hesitation, reluctance, no motivation.
        • The normal flow of motivation is disturbed, so we cannot wait to feel motivated.
        • The initial step must be taken deliberately. We have to force ourselves to initiate the action.

        Why is the first step always mine?

        • The fear is not necessarily about the Y-type idea at all. It is about my existing conditioning and its clash with the idea.
        • I may truly want it and know it is good for me, and still feel resistance. The difficulty of starting comes from the resistance, not from the activity.
        • Even with a harmonious idea I am the one who acts. The only difference is that motivation is already there. So in the X–Y situation, I take the first step despite Chaos Vibration.

        6

        How to actually take action in the moment

        The aim is to make the work behind our goals part of everyday routine. That routine is made of single tasks, so the real question is small: how do I get up and do this one task while I feel fear, hesitation and reluctance? Whatever the answer is, it works for the second task, the third, and every one after.

        The four principles

        1My initiative will turn into a routineTap to open

        The step I am about to take is part of cultivating my routine. I already have the method: the sessions, the activity, the repetition.

        I remind myself that my initiative will contribute to the routine I want, then I leave the question of routine formation alone.

        2Understand the fear and turn a blind eyeTap to open

        The fear is connected to my X-type conditioning and its clash with the Y-type idea. It is not necessarily about the activity. It does not mean the activity is bad, or that I do not want it.

        Turn a blind eye: acknowledge the feeling, but refuse to let it decide an activity I have already judged worthwhile and safe. This means willingness to act despite discomfort, not disregard for real harm, punishment or responsibilities.

        3My intention is to engage in the activityTap to open

        I do not focus on whether I feel motivated, on the fear, or on how hard it might be. I focus on doing the activity itself: get up, prepare, begin the session. When it is done, move to the next.

        My focus is on the action, not on the reluctance before it.

        4Affirm yourself for courageTap to open

        Courage does not mean the absence of fear. Courage means taking action despite fear. I do not wait to become fearless or comfortable.

        I tell myself: “Let’s just do this and see what happens. Jo hoga dekh lenge.” Whatever reasonable challenge comes, we will deal with it.

        7

        Retrieval paragraph

        The Retrieval Paragraph

        To be read at the exact moment when I need to begin an activity and am experiencing fear, hesitation, or reluctance.

        I know what is happening right now. I have decided to do an activity, and I am feeling fear, hesitation, and reluctance. But I already understand this process. This resistance is coming from the clash between my X-type conditioning and the Y-type idea. It is Chaos Vibration at work. This fear is not about the Y-type idea itself. It does not mean that the activity is bad for me, that I do not want it, or that I should avoid it. This fear is natural, innate to the process, and simply part of how my existing conditioning reacts to something new. So I can ignore it. I do not need to give it any importance. I also know that my initiative right now is contributing to the routine I want to cultivate. So I am turning a blind eye to this fear, hesitation, and reluctance. My intention right now is simply to engage in the activity. That is what I want to do, and that is where my focus belongs. I know that the first step has to be mine, and I am willing to take it. Let's just do this and see what happens. جو ہوگا دیکھ لیں گے۔ Whatever comes afterward, we will deal with it, InshaAllah.
