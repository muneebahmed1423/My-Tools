The False Question of Choice

A confusion that looks like open-mindedness

There is a kind of confusion that looks like open-mindedness. It says there are many ways to live and that you must choose among them. It is true that people live in many different ways. But the conclusion drawn from that fact does not follow: that all these ways are genuine options, that they are equally available to you, and that selecting among them is a neutral act. That conclusion is a trap.

“Lifestyle choice”

The phrase assumes that truth is separate from the way you live, and that your way of living is a personal preference, like choosing a paint colour for your room.

A misunderstanding of truth

Putting why humans exist and what life is for on the same level as ordinary tastes is not real open-mindedness. It is a misunderstanding of what truth is.

The right path

Not a cultural preference. It is a description of what you actually are and what you were made for as a human being.

Taste or truth?

Switch between the two kinds of question

The fish and the water

The truth of your existence is not an opinion. Treating the right path as one lifestyle option among many is as confused as a fish treating water as one habitat option among many.

A fish, its water and its swimming

How the trap works

The framing of choice is not only intellectually wrong but spiritually dangerous. It does not announce itself. Follow it step by step.

What Is the Right Path?

Two ways a human being can live

The right path is a concept in Islamic theology, which is the religion I follow. It says that there are two ways a human being can live. One is to become a Muslim and live as a Muslim. The other is to remain a non-Muslim and live as a non-Muslim.

The right path

The path a human being can walk that leads straight to paradise. A very straight path. Its Arabic name is Sirat-e-Mustaqim.

The deviant path

Anyone who is not on the right path is on the deviant path, which can be called the wrong path.

Real success

Success in the afterlife, which means reaching paradise.

The straight path and the deviant path

Questions that come up

Two or three questions come up when we talk about the right path in the context of Muslims and non-Muslims. To walk on it a person needs to be a Muslim, with faith in God and in all that Islam says. But what about non-Muslims who are happy and content, or even spiritual? What about the People of the Book, such as Christians and Jews?

1What about non-Muslims who are happy, content or even spiritual?In my own understanding

First I need to say that I am an ignorant person and I do not know everything. But according to my knowledge and understanding, some non-Muslims do feel a void within themselves, and that void is filled when they revert to Islam. Others did not get that chance. Allah Almighty did not give it to them, so they became unresponsive to the void within, unconscious of it, and stopped feeling it even though it is still there.

That does not mean it is something they are constantly worried about. They can still feel joy and happiness. But real contentment and real satisfaction may be something they have no idea about or feel no need for. That is a very unfortunate thing to go through, and Allah Almighty made it part of their destiny. This raises another question, about destiny versus choice, but that is a separate debate. The bottom line is that everything lies in Allah’s hand.

2If people are not aware of the right path, how can they be concerned with it?The path is a description of your nature

The right path is the path explained in Islam, and it is in harmony with the very nature of human beings, whether a person is Muslim or not. The right path is a description of your nature. But that does not mean people are necessarily aware of it, and if they are not aware of it, how can they be concerned with it?

The quest

The Right Path as the Ground You Are Standing On

Not a destination

The right path is not a destination. It is not something ahead of you, not something reached after enough preparation, not a future project. It is a ground: something you are either standing on right now or failing to stand on right now. It exists beneath you, not in front of you, and it is available in this present moment, requiring nothing but that you place your weight on it.

The ground beneath you

The only meaningful question

A present-tense one: “Am I standing on it right now?”

The wrong question

The future-tense one: “When will I get there?”

What the ground settles

Where he stands.

What it leaves open

Every step from there. He still walks, builds, earns and decides what to do with his day.

Standing on the ground is not the same as standing still.

4

What Is Fitrah?

The self that is already on the ground

Fitrah is identified with this ground itself. It is the natural, real, deep, original self, and this self is already on the right path. It does not need to travel to it or be built into it. Fitrah is not neutral raw material waiting to be shaped into righteousness. It is a self that already knows what it is, already recognises the truth, and already inclines toward what is real.

Fitrah and its coverings · tap a layer

The work of the right path is uncovering, not constructing. You are removing what has been layered over a self that was always whole and already aligned. It is a matter of removal and recognition, not of achievement or arrival.

A want that grows out of the self

It is part of the self.

A want laid over it

It is not part of the self. It is a covering.

Notice what this does not say. It does not say that every want a person has is a covering. Telling the two apart is something the later sections depend on (see section 6).

6

The True Question of Choice

What is left to choose?

The false question asks which way of life to pick. The true question begins only after that one has been answered, and it is a far more ordinary question: given that I am standing on the ground, what do I do with my life from here?

The false question

Which path to stand on. Its answer is fixed.

The true question

What to do while standing on it. Here a human being is free, so long as Layer A stays above Layer B.

The difficulty

Take someone who wants to become a businessman or a sportsman. These are personal ambitions, and they carry a real desire for achievement. A sportsman gives people quality entertainment, a businessman provides a service, and benefiting the creation of Allah pleases Him. That is true, but it does not settle the matter. If the pursuit comes from a personal desire and a sense of achievement, how can it be devotion to Allah and not simply self-interest? The difficulty is in linking a personal aspiration to a higher purpose in line with Allah’s will.

The fish shows where the answer lies. It has no choice about water, and a great deal of choice about where in the water it swims. The matter is layered.

Two layers in every action

Layer A is the ground. Layer B is the walking done on it.

What a desire must pass through

Layer B is not unlimited. A desire in this layer has to meet two conditions. First, it must not be inherently wrong; wrong desires are those explicitly prohibited in Islamic teaching. Second, it must not grow out of a negative emotion. Desires driven by excessive fear, anger, revenge or jealousy are also improper.

Try it · test a desire

Once a desire has passed both conditions, nothing more is demanded of it. A person who wants financial stability is free to choose his profession, whether as a sportsman, an accountant or a lawyer. The choice of a spouse is likewise a personal one. These decisions fall under individual rights, and those rights must be acknowledged and fulfilled.

The rights of the self

Just as a person has duties toward Allah and toward others, he also has rights over himself, and honouring those rights is part of keeping balance in life. The nafs has its rights, and they need to be fulfilled.

A desire that has been examined, tested against the two conditions and then chosen is a right. An impulse simply obeyed in the moment is the nafs taking over a decision that belonged to the person: a covering pretending to be Layer B.

The attitude to life

Do everything for Allah Almighty while leaving room for personal desires in the areas where they belong. There will be moments of pursuing what one wants. What matters is to remember that desire never takes precedence over devotion. All worship, obligations and acts of devotion belong to Allah alone. In the matter of desires a person is free, within what is right, but those desires must never overshadow the greater purpose.

Summary

There is one right path, and it is not an option among many. It is the ground of your own nature. Fitrah is the original self already standing on that ground, and the work is to uncover that self, not to build a new one. Standing on the ground does not remove the freedom to walk. A person does everything for Allah (Layer A) and still has room for right, examined desires (Layer B).

The choice of path is false. The choice of what to do on it is real.

On Barakah and the Fallen State

If you are not on the right path, there is no barakah and no divine help. That is the rule. For non-Muslims not on a “right path” in the Islamic sense, good work and hard work still get rewarded in this world, because Allah is Adil (just) and does not wrong anyone. Beyond that general principle, the deeper mechanics of how that compensation works are not fully known here. Allah knows best.

For a Muslim in a fallen state, two scenarios are possible.

Work still gets done, without barakah

Scenario 1

Work still gets done, without barakah or divine help. It means Allah is extending more time to stand up and return to the right path.

Scenario 2

Work does not get done at all. The more direct and simpler manifestation of having no blessings, no help, and no barakah. It is dangerous in its own right.

17. The overall lesson of the non-Fitrah period

The right path is not something to reach later. It is the ground to stand on now.

When deviation occurs, do these in order:

  • Repent immediately
  • Remain sinful rather than hopeless
  • Stop further regression
  • Refuse negotiation with the nafs
  • Recognize whispers
  • Cross the terror barrier
  • Prevent bad patterns from becoming established
  • Cement the correct patterns gradually, through repeated present-moment action

The objectiveThe objective is not simply to stop sinning. It is to restore the complete Fitrah-aligned life: Qurb-e-Ilahi, taqwa, Islamic identity, low wahn, disciplined growth, meaningful work, physical health, and a living bond with Islam.
