How this works

  • Maintenance is your resting calories (Mifflin–St Jeor equation) times an activity multiplier. In reviews this equation was the one most often within 10% of lab measurements, but individual errors can be bigger. Treat it as a starting point and adjust after 2–3 weeks of tracking your average weight.
  • Fat loss uses your target weight and timeline, then caps the pace at 1% of body weight per week, a deficit of at most 25% of maintenance, and a floor of 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men). The timeline is recalculated week by week because maintenance falls as you get lighter; the old fixed “3,500 kcal per pound” rule overestimates weight loss.
  • Protein is set higher in a deficit (about 2.0 g/kg, range 1.8–2.4) to protect muscle, together with resistance training. It is capped at 35% of your calories. Fat is at least 25% of calories and 0.6 g/kg. Carbs fill the rest.
  • Bulking: lean bulk is about 10% above maintenance (about +150 to +350 kcal), fast bulk about 20% (about +400 to +700 kcal), the one people call a “dirty bulk”. Bigger surpluses speed up gain, but in trained people most of the extra goes to fat. Weight gained is not all muscle.
  • Safety: this tool is for healthy adults. If you are under 18, pregnant or breastfeeding, have a medical condition, take medication that affects weight, or have a history of disordered eating, get advice from a doctor or registered dietitian first. These are estimates, not medical advice.
